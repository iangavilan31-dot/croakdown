# Vision

> Part of the [[00 START HERE|CROAKDOWN bible]] · source: `docs/VISION.md` (Ian, verbatim) + [[Master Directive]] · updated 2026-07-07

## The promise

**Every melee attack should feel so satisfying that players genuinely enjoy swinging their weapon even when there is absolutely no reward.** The combat alone should be fun. Everything else exists to amplify that feeling.

CROAKDOWN is **the most satisfying melee horde survivor ever made** — not "Brotato with frogs." The player constantly feels themselves becoming an unstoppable swamp guardian cutting through enormous swarms in a mysterious bioluminescent night swamp.

## The thirty-second test

Two friends in Discord late at night. They boot the game. Within thirty seconds one says *"dude… this feels incredible."* What they noticed, in order:

1. **Weapon weight** — the greatsword drags, winds up, and lands like a falling tree
2. **Physics** — enemies launch, bounce, tumble, slide through mud, collide with each other
3. **Impact** — hitstop, flash, knockback, splatter, sound
4. **Enemy reactions** — everything flinches, staggers, gushes, dies expressively
5. **Satisfying movement** — hop cadence, dash-hop, tongue pulls
6. **Expressive animation** — nothing slides, everything breathes

If a playtester's first comment is about content or progression instead, the combat has failed and we stop adding anything until it's fixed. This is the [[Quality Gates|Swing Test]] gate in code form.

## The feeling targets

- **Player**: "I am becoming ridiculously powerful." Power must be *visible on the frog* ([[Progression and Evolution]]) and *legible in the swarm* (bigger arcs, bigger launches, denser splatter).
- **Swamp**: alive, beautiful, ancient, mysterious, dangerous. Another character, never a backdrop ([[Environment and Reactivity]]).
- **By wave 20 the arena visually tells the story of the run** — bones, blood, broken reeds, glowing relics, hundreds of fallen enemies. Permanence is a feature ([[VFX Standards]]).

## Co-op is the native mode

Built for co-op from day one, not single-player with two people. The best stories should happen naturally — a tongue-pull into a partner's hammer swing, a last-second revive under a boss, an argument over the shared wallet. See [[Co-op Design]].
